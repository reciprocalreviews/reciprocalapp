# Volunteering to review

Venues on Reciprocal Reviews define **roles**: reviewer, program committee member, area
chair, and so on. Volunteering means telling a venue which roles you are willing to take
and how much you are willing to do.

## Bidding for a role

On a venue's page, each open role shows what it involves and what it pays, if the venue
uses tokens. For each one you can express a preference, typically something like
_preferred_, _if necessary_, or _no_, and say how many submissions you are willing to
handle.

Some roles are **invite only**. You will not see a bidding option for those; instead an
editor invites you, and you accept or decline.

## Being assigned

Editors do the assigning, not the platform. They use your stated preferences and capacity
as input, along with expertise and conflicts of interest. You are emailed when you are
assigned a submission, and again if you are removed from one.

Reciprocal Reviews does not host the reviews themselves. The actual reviewing happens in
whatever system the venue already uses.

## Getting compensated

If the venue uses tokens, you request compensation after doing the work by following the
link in the venue's own emails back to Reciprocal Reviews. Compensation is **pull-based**:
nobody watches for you finishing and pays you automatically, so if you don't ask, you
don't get paid.

## Declaring a conflict

If you are assigned something you should not review, declare a conflict of interest on the
submission rather than quietly declining. That tells the editor why.
