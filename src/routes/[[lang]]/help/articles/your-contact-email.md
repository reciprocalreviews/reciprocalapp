# Your contact email

You sign in to Reciprocal Reviews with your **ORCID iD**, not an email address. ORCID does
not give us an email, so if you want notifications you have to tell us where to send them.

## Verifying it

Add your address on your profile. We send a verification link to it, and the address is
only stored once you follow that link. Until then we send you nothing except that one
verification email, and a banner reminds you that notifications are off.

The link is valid for **24 hours** and is only ever sent by email. We never show it on
screen, because a link you could read on screen would prove nothing about who controls the
address. Clicking it more than once within the window is harmless, so a mail scanner or
link preview cannot use it up before you do.

Until you follow it, your profile says which address is waiting and when the link stops
working, with a **Send it again** button beside it. You will find the same button on the
page an expired link lands on, so a lapsed link is one click from a fresh one rather than a
trip back to your profile to retype an address. There is no limit on asking again, though
the button waits a minute between requests.

## Why you might not be getting email

- **The address was never verified.** Check your profile for the warning banner.
- **The verification link expired.** Use **Send it again**, on your profile or on the page
  the expired link lands on. There is no limit.
- **The verification email itself never went out.** If something went wrong on our side
  rather than yours, your profile says so instead of telling you a link is on its way —
  check the address for a typo, and tell the stewards if it keeps happening.
- **It went to spam.** Our mail comes from `notifications@reciprocal.reviews`. Marking it
  as not-spam helps.
- **You changed your address** but never confirmed the new one. Changing it uses the same
  verification flow, and until you complete it, the old address stays in place.

## Changing or removing it

Change it on your profile at any time, using the same verification flow. Your email is
only ever a contact address, never your identity here, so changing it cannot collide with
anyone else's account.
